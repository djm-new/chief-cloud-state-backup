[link] Save article: THE CLOCKS ON ONE BUILDING — Shanaka Anslem Perera, Substack, 2026-08-08

URL: https://open.substack.com/pub/shanakaanslemperera/p/the-clocks-on-one-building?r=2ydh2&utm_medium=ios

Why it matters: The article frames Meta’s AI/data-center financings as one physical campus governed by multiple overlapping institutional clocks: four-year initial leases, extension options up to twenty years, sixteen-year residual-value guarantees, debt running to 2049, plus separate power, grid, regulatory, operational, insolvency, and workload-continuity timelines.

Core thesis: one building is not one obligation. The building, lease, guarantee, debt, power right, workload, and regulated service each bind different parties on different calendars. The important question is not which clock is “true”; it is which clock becomes binding first.

Key analytical split to preserve: accounting control, creditor recovery, physical succession, and workload continuity are four different outcomes and must be underwritten separately. A residual-value guarantee can protect creditors before any market-clearing transaction reveals what the unsupported campus is actually worth.

Meta examples captured: El Paso, Texas structure with BlackRock-managed funds, roughly $14B development cost, $12.5B debt, 80/20 external-partner/Meta ownership, Meta as construction manager and full-campus tenant, four-year initial leases with options up to twenty years, and a declining residual-value guarantee through the first sixteen years. Richland Parish / Hyperion, Louisiana uses a similar structure with Blue Owl-managed funds, roughly $27B development cost, scheduled lease commencement in 2029, four-year initial lease, options up to twenty years, and an approximately $28B declining residual-value guarantee threshold.

Memorable line: “The building is one object only from a distance. Up close, it is a stack of clocks waiting for different contracts, markets and authorities to say that time has run out.”
