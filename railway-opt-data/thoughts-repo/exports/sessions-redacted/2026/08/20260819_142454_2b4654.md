## Main thesis

A data center may look like one asset, but analytically it is a stack of separate instruments and risks:

> “The building is not the lease. The lease is not the guarantee. The guarantee is not the debt. The debt is not the power right. The power right is not the workload.”

The key question is not “what is the true timeline?” but:

> **Which clock becomes binding first?**

## Why it matters

The essay warns against treating Meta’s guarantees or lease options as if they solve every risk. They may protect creditors in one scenario, but they do not necessarily solve:

- **Accounting/control risk** — who actually controls the asset economically?
- **Creditor recovery risk** — will lenders get paid on time?
- **Physical succession risk** — can another tenant/operator actually use the campus?
- **Workload continuity risk** — can the AI/digital service keep running elsewhere?
- **Regulatory/power risk** — do grid, energy, and public obligations outlive the lease?

## The Meta examples

The piece discusses Meta’s repeated structure:

**El Paso, Texas**
- Around **$14B** development cost

A residual-value guarantee may protect lenders or investors, but it does not prove the building itself has that value in the open market.
