Totally fair — that notation is Unix **cron syntax**. It’s compact but not human-friendly.

It has 5 fields:

``​`text
minute hour day-of-month month day-of-week
``​`

So:

``​`text
15 7 * * *
``​`

means:

- minute: `15`
- hour: `7`
- day-of-month: `*` = every day
- month: `*` = every month
- day-of-week: `*` = every weekday/weekend

In plain English: **every day at 7:15 AM UTC**.

For our current jobs:

- `15 7 * * *`  
  → every day at **7:15 AM UTC**

- `0 */3 * * *`  
  → every **3 hours**, on the hour  
  → 00:00, 03:00, 06:00, 09:00, etc. UTC

- `0 12 * * *`  
  → every day at **12:00 PM UTC**

- `0 9,10 * * *`  
  → every day at **9:00 AM and 10:00 AM UTC**  
  → this is used for the 5AM ET ToM job because Eastern Time shifts with daylight savings; the script itself only does work when the current time is actually 5AM ET.

- `55 12,20 * * *`  
  → every day at **12:55 PM and 8:55 PM UTC**

- `5 13,21 * * *`  
  → every day at **1:05 PM and 9:05 PM UTC**

`*` just means “every possible value” for that field.
