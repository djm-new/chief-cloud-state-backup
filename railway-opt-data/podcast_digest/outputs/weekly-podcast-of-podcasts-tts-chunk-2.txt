Which brings us to the ROI reckoning. Matan Grinberg on *The Twenty Minute VC* made the point that token budgets are the new capital allocation. You’re not just choosing which model to use—you’re choosing how much to spend, and on what kind of inference. And that’s forcing companies to think like investors: what’s the return on each token spent?

And it’s not just about cost. It’s about strategy. Grinberg argues that open source is going to replace most frontier model work. That’s a big call. But if you look at what’s happening with Llama, Mistral, and others, the economics are hard to ignore. You don’t need a $10B model if an open-source one can do 80% of the job for 10% of the cost.

Which brings us to the platform play. Because if the model is becoming a commodity, then the differentiator is how you productize it. And that’s where Apple and Microsoft come in. Stratechery this week compared Fable to Apple Intelligence, and it’s a useful contrast. Apple is taking the consumer platform angle—embedding AI into the OS, making it invisible but indispensable.

And that’s the endgame for a lot of these tools. It’s not about having the biggest model. It’s about having the best integration. The most seamless UX. The most defensible platform. That’s the Microsoft/Apple playbook: take the infrastructure, wrap it in a product, and make it feel native. That’s where the operating leverage kicks in.

So what does all this mean for someone like DJ? You’re not just watching a tech shift—you’re seeing a capital shift. From compute to governance, from models to margins. The winners will be the ones who treat AI not as a research project, but as a business problem.

Exactly. The era of model novelty is over. Now it’s about governance, utility, and ROI. The companies that survive—and thrive—are the ones who can turn AI into infrastructure, productize it effectively, and allocate capital smartly across the stack.
