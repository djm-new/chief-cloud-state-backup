### Episode 1: *The Cognitive Revolution* – Dean Ball on Joining OpenAI

MAYA: First up is *The Cognitive Revolution*, featuring Dean Ball discussing his move to OpenAI to lead frontier AI policy. He previously helped draft the U.S. AI Action Plan and reflects on its implementation, export controls, and the risks of government monopolizing frontier AI.

SAM: What’s the main thesis here?

MAYA: Dean argues that centralizing frontier AI control in government hands—like the DoD labeling Anthropic a supply chain risk—makes policymaking brittle. He’s pushing for a more distributed, transparent approach, citing state-level transparency laws and independent verification bodies as promising models.

SAM: What’s his strongest argument?

MAYA: He draws a sharp distinction between the U.S. AI Action Plan’s lofty goals and its real-world execution. He admits it’s more a list of objectives than a cohesive strategy, and that implementation has drifted—like the rushed export controls on frontier models that alienated international partners.

SAM: Any counterpoints or open questions?

MAYA: Dean acknowledges that while government oversight is necessary, the current approach—like the NSA’s classified pre-deployment testing—risks creating a black box around AI safety and capability. He worries this secrecy undermines public trust and accountability.

SAM: Why should DJ care?

MAYA: Because Dean is now inside OpenAI shaping policy at the frontier. His perspective will influence how frontier labs interact with regulators, especially around transparency and export controls. DJ should watch how OpenAI balances internal deployment decisions with external regulation.

### Episode 2: *Latent Space* – Anjney Midha on AI Compute Efficiency

MAYA: Next is *Latent Space*, where Anjney Midha talks about founding AMP and the importance of maximizing AI compute efficiency. He dives into MFU (Model FLOPs Utilization), infrastructure waste, and the vision for an independent compute grid.

SAM: What’s the main thesis?

MAYA: Anjney argues that AI compute waste is massive—some frontier labs are running below 10% MFU, while best-in-class is 60–70%. He believes the future of AI infrastructure lies in aligning systems, scheduling, and utilization to maximize output, not just buying more GPUs.

SAM: Strongest argument?

MAYA: He compares frontier AI to a systems problem, not just a hardware or model problem. He explains how Google historically achieved 95% node utilization, and how frontier labs are falling short—highlighting that simply buying more GPUs won’t solve the underlying inefficiencies.

SAM: What’s the counterpoint?

MAYA: There’s a tension between pushing for full-stack alignment and the reality of fragmented infrastructure. Anjney admits that while AMP is building an independent compute grid, the current landscape is dominated by a few players—NVIDIA, Google, and soon, maybe Elon Musk and Intel with Terrafab.

SAM: Why should DJ care?

MAYA: Because Anjney is not just an observer—he’s a key investor in frontier AI companies like Anthropic and now founder of AMP. His insights on compute efficiency and alignment directly influence how capital and infrastructure are allocated in the AI stack.

### Episode 3: *No Priors* – Lip Bu Tan on Transforming Intel

MAYA: Finally, *No Priors* brings us Lip Bu Tan, CEO of Intel, discussing his turnaround strategy, the Terrafab collaboration with Elon Musk, and the future of U.S. semiconductor infrastructure.

SAM: What’s the main thesis?

MAYA: Tan believes Intel must crawl before it can run—focusing on product excellence, yield improvements, and rebuilding trust with customers. He’s doubling down on the foundry business despite its capital intensity, arguing it’s critical for U.S. supply chain resilience.

SAM: Strongest argument?

MAYA: He makes a compelling case that the U.S. needs a robust domestic semiconductor ecosystem. He cites TSMC’s success with government support and argues that Intel’s foundry business is essential for national security and global competitiveness.

SAM: Counterpoint?

MAYA: There’s skepticism about whether Intel can catch up with TSMC, especially in advanced nodes like 14A (~1.4nm). Tan admits they’re lagging but insists that yield, defect density, and cycle time improvements will close the gap.

SAM: Why should DJ care?

MAYA: Because Tan is betting on a full-stack AI future—silicon, packaging, software, and systems. DJ should pay attention to how Intel positions itself in the AI inference market and how its collaboration with Musk on Terrafab could reshape silicon capacity for AI and robotics.
