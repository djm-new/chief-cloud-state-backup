## Must listen

### 1. Satya Nadella on Microsoft’s AI platform strategy

**Where:** Stratechery / No Priors / Latent Space crossover cluster  
**Guest:** Satya Nadella, CEO of Microsoft  
**Recommendation:** **Must listen** — choose the Stratechery interview if you want strategy; choose No Priors/Latent Space if you want broader AI-platform/product discussion.

**Why DJ should care:** This is the cleanest articulation I found today of how a trillion-dollar incumbent thinks about AI as a platform shift. Nadella is not framing Microsoft’s advantage as “we have the best model.” He is framing it as enterprise trust, multi-model harnesses, private evals, distribution, and turning each company’s tacit knowledge into its own “hill-climbing machine.”

**Key ideas:**

- Microsoft’s role may be less “own the one frontier model” and more “trusted platform for every enterprise to run its own learning system.”
- Nadella’s phrase: companies will have **human capital** and **token capital**.
- The new enterprise moat may be **private evals + private outputs + tacit knowledge captured in AI systems**.
- Software is not dead, but pricing shifts from pure per-seat SaaS toward entitlement + usage/consumption because agents create marginal compute costs.
- Microsoft is managing dependence on OpenAI by building its own MAI model lineage and multi-model infrastructure.
- AI capex discipline matters: Nadella explicitly distinguishes hyperscale demand, Microsoft app demand, and research/model demand.

**Action/toolkit implication:** Start thinking of AI adoption less as “which tools do we buy?” and more as “what private evals and workflows become our hill-climbing machine?” That is a concrete operating lens.

### 2. David Solomon on running Goldman Sachs in the age of AI

**Where:** Odd Lots  
**Guest:** David Solomon, CEO of Goldman Sachs  
**Recommendation:** **Must listen / digest plus maybe full listen**

**Why DJ should care:** This is an actual large-enterprise AI deployment conversation, not abstract AI futurism. Goldman is a good prism because it has tech budgets, regulated workflows, high-value human relationships, back-office process work, and junior knowledge-worker pipelines.

**Key ideas:**

- Solomon is highly optimistic on AI productivity but rejects the simple white-collar-job-apocalypse narrative.
- Clean data is the binding constraint: “garbage in, garbage out” remains true for AI.
- KYC/onboarding/AML processes are the obvious automation targets — thousands of people touching processes can become hundreds.
- Senior relationship work may become more valuable, not less, because trust, EQ, judgment, and client intimacy are harder to automate.
- Goldman still plans to hire large analyst/intern classes, though entry-level hiring may contract somewhat.
- His view of investment banking remains relationship-compounding: SpaceX was “won over twenty years,” not in six months.
- Public markets may end up with fewer, larger public companies; companies will wait longer to go public.

**Action/toolkit implication:** Useful framework: split AI impact by workflow type:

1. Clean-data process work — automate aggressively.  
2. Judgment/relationship work — augment, don’t replace.  
3. Junior apprenticeship work — redesign, because this is where the old training ladder may break.

**Link:** https://omny.fm/shows/odd-lots/goldman-sachs-ceo-david-solomon-on-running-a-bank-in-the-age-of-ai

## Digest only

### 5. Dwarkesh: What remains scarce after AGI?

**Where:** Dwarkesh Podcast  
**Guests:** Alex Imas and Phil Trammell  
**Recommendation:** **Digest only unless you want deep AGI economics**

**Why DJ should care:** This is not an operator conversation, but it is exactly the kind of framework conversation that can change how you think. The core question is: after AGI, what remains scarce, and therefore where does value accrue?

**Key ideas:**

- “Relational sector”: goods/services where human involvement is part of the value.
- Labor share vs capital share may be transformed if automated supply chains push capital share toward one.
- Private value capture depends on whether AI looks more like electricity, where gains diffuse, or social media, where platforms capture rents.
- Developing countries outside the AI supply chain are an under-discussed problem.
- They call for much better data — a “Manhattan Project for data” around AI economics.

**Action/toolkit implication:** Useful framework: when evaluating any AI-disrupted market, ask what remains scarce: compute, data, distribution, trust, human presence, regulatory position, or capital?

**Link:** https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/alex-imas-phil-trammell
