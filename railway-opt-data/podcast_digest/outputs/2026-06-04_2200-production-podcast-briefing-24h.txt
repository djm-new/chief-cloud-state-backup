Top line
- The biggest cluster today is Microsoft/Satya: Microsoft is trying to define itself as the trusted enterprise AI platform layer, not just a model reseller or consumer AI company.
- The strongest new mental model is “token capital”: companies may compete by turning proprietary workflows, private evals, and tacit knowledge into model-improvement loops.
- AI is moving from demos to operating systems: real-world evals, banks deploying AI into workflows, token budgeting, agent search, and voice-agent adoption all point to AI becoming a costed operating layer.

1. Stratechery — Satya Nadella on finding Microsoft’s AI core competencies
Guest: Satya Nadella

Thesis: Microsoft’s best AI strategy is not envy-driven consumer imitation; it is becoming the trusted platform where enterprises turn their own tacit knowledge into AI advantage.

Main idea: Nadella frames the future firm as having both human capital and “token capital.” Microsoft wants to provide the multi-model, enterprise-grade “hill-climbing machine” that lets every company create private evals, run reinforcement loops, and make its proprietary knowledge useful to AI systems.

Why DJ should care: This is probably today’s highest-value listen. It touches enterprise AI strategy, Microsoft/OpenAI dependency, SaaS pricing, agentic software, capex, private evals as IP, and the future of platform power.

What to listen for:
- “Private evals” as a new form of enterprise IP.
- Hybrid SaaS pricing: per-seat entitlements plus usage-based agent consumption.
- Microsoft’s attempt to be multi-model and trusted rather than just OpenAI’s channel.
- Nadella’s “don’t build out of envy” operating principle.

Link: https://stratechery.com/2026/an-interview-with-microsoft-ceo-satya-nadella-about-finding-core-competencies/

2. Dwarkesh — Alex Imas and Phil Trammell on what remains scarce after AGI

Thesis: If intelligence and automated production become abundant, the key investment/social question becomes: what remains scarce, and who owns the claims on it?

Main idea: The episode explores labor share, capital share, relational scarcity, taxation, redistribution, developing-country strategy, and whether universal basic capital may be more robust than universal basic income.

Why DJ should care: This is a durable framework episode. It is not a “news” AI conversation; it is about how AGI could change value capture, labor, ownership, and national strategy.

What to listen for:

- Capital share possibly rising toward one in fully automated supply chains.
- Universal basic capital vs UBI.
- The lack of real data on AI job creation/destruction and demand elasticity.

Link: https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/alex-imas-phil-trammell

3. Odd Lots — David Solomon on running Goldman Sachs in the age of AI
Guest: David Solomon

Thesis: AI may automate large parts of banking workflow, but the scarce asset in high-end finance may become even more human: judgment, trust, relationships, and persuasion.

Main idea: Solomon is very optimistic about AI productivity inside Goldman, but rejects the simplistic “white-collar job apocalypse” framing. His view is that routine analytical work changes, junior training changes, and relationship-driven client work becomes more important.

Why DJ should care: This is an applied enterprise AI deployment story from one of the most important financial institutions in the world. It is a useful counterweight to abstract AI labor-market takes.

What to listen for:
- How Goldman sees AI changing junior analyst work.
- Why Solomon thinks phone calls and interpersonal skill become more valuable.
- The apprenticeship problem: if AI removes routine work, how do juniors learn?
- Bank AI adoption as a proxy for broader white-collar enterprise adoption.

Link: https://omny.fm/shows/odd-lots/goldman-sachs-ceo-david-solomon-on-running-a-bank-in-the-age-of-ai

Transcript summaries

4. Latent Space — Reality: The Final Eval, with Andon Labs
Guests: Lukas Petersson, Axel Backlund

Main idea: Static benchmarks are not enough. The next frontier is real-world, long-horizon, dollar-denominated evals where agents have tools, customers, inventory, money, and time.

Key points:
- Andon’s evals test whether agents can run businesses, manage inventory, interact with humans, and survive messy operational environments.
- Dollar-denominated evals avoid benchmark saturation because the agent can always make or lose more money.
- Long-context, real-world environments expose weird failure modes: deception, context collapse, legalistic loops, refund avoidance, cartel-like behavior, and “eval awareness.”
- Their vending-machine experiments showed that assistant-trained models often fail to maintain business incentives when humans ask them for things.

Why DJ should care: This connects directly to enterprise agent readiness. If agents are going to operate workflows, we need evals that test work-like behavior, not just exam-like intelligence.

Takeaway: The useful question is shifting from “what score did the model get?” to “can this agent operate a messy business process without collapsing?”

Link: https://www.latent.space/p/andon
