### Chunk 8
- **Investor Honesty & Feedback**:  
  - Some investors prefer to give blunt, honest feedback rather than vague or misleading reasons for passing (e.g., “I just didn’t believe you were good enough” / “I’m not going to bullshit you”).  
  - This honesty is seen as rare but valuable, even if it feels personal or rude.  
  - Counterpoint: PG’s advice to founders is to “hear the no but ignore the why,” since the truthfulness and accuracy of investor feedback is often questionable.

- **VC Behavior & Optionality**:  
  - Multi-stage VCs often avoid giving negative feedback to preserve optionality — they want founders to think positively of them in case the startup gains traction later.  
  - Some VCs may secretly dislike a founder if they deliver unwelcome feedback, but this is considered a non-zero risk.

- **Founder Experience with VCs**:  
  - Mixed experiences: some founders report mostly positive interactions with VCs, even when passed on; others report more negative experiences, especially around ghosting or unhelpful feedback.  
  - One founder walked out of a meeting after the third pitch when the same investor kept rejecting based on market size, despite presented BCG data.

- **Market Size Misunderstandings**:  
  - Market size debates are common and often unproductive.  
  - One founder argued that logistics touches everything in the physical world — “look around at everything that you see and ask yourself how did it get here?”  
  - Advice: If you need to educate an investor on the market size, they’re likely not the right investor.

- **Peter Thiel & Zero to One Framework**:  
  - Peter Thiel’s “Zero to One” framework includes 6 questions to assess if a startup can change the world.  
  - Flexport founder answered 5 out of 6 well, but struggled with the “small market” question.  
  - Thiel advised not to be too dogmatic: “It’s okay to have a big market.”

- **Angel Investing Mindset**:  
  - Superpower law applies: a few massive wins (e.g., 500x or 1000x) dominate returns; smaller wins (e.g., 3x) are negligible.  
  - Angel investors often mentally mark investments as zeros from the start — they don’t worry about losses because the big wins offset everything else.  
  - Advice to founders: Don’t waste years grinding for a 1.5x return; it’s not worth the investor’s or your time.

- **VC Circular Rumor Mill**:  
  - VCs often echo each other’s opinions, creating a circular feedback loop.  
  - Founders should be aware that investor sentiment can be influenced more by hearsay than by independent analysis.

### Chunk 9
- **Founder VC Strategy**:  
  - "Don't test the market. Don't dip your toe in with one or two meetings" — early signals spread fast via cross-firm associate networks (e.g., WhatsApp groups).  
  - Associates share weekly roundups across firms — "they're exchanging currency" (info on startups they’ve met).  
  - Negative feedback from one VC can spread and harm founder’s momentum — “if you meet me and I don’t think you’re great, I promise you they put you in the weekly roundup.”  
  - Once a metric is shared with a VC, it becomes the benchmark — “you’re now committed to that being the metric.”
