### Chunk 4
- **Startups in the AI ecosystem**:  
  - There's no significant gap in model quality, but rather in understanding how to build great games (e.g., proper scaffolding).  
  - Some startups are already doing this right, but there's a discoverability and awareness problem.  
  - Certain model capabilities are only weeks or months away from crossing the chasm to mainstream usability.

- **World models vs. coding agents for game development**:  
  - Short-term: Vibe-coded games are more likely to be powered by **coding agents + game engines**, not world models.  
  - Long-term: World models may become viable for games in ~2 years.  
  - Current world models are not well-suited for games due to open-endedness and lack of grounding in specific use cases.  
  - Startups are exploring scaffolding to make world models usable in practical contexts like gaming.

- **World model definitions are blurry**:  
  - **Omni** (Google's multimodal model) is an example of this blurriness — it’s being framed as a world model due to its deep understanding of the world.  
  - Omni differs architecturally from traditional world models like Genie, which were action-conditioned video models.  
  - Traditional world models were not scalable; Omni is more scalable and functional, though not yet real-time.  
  - The line between world models and video models is becoming less clear — Omni can perform similar visual tasks as traditional world models.

- **Omni's architecture and capabilities**:  
  - Omni is a **single model**, not a routing system across multiple models.  
  - Previously, Google used multiple models (e.g., Gemini for text, Lyria for music, etc.).  
  - Omni Flash is the first iteration; more powerful versions are in development.  
  - Current public use case: **video editing** (e.g., real-time editing of a talk with a dog entering the stage, including realistic reactions from attendees).  
  - Omni preserves the original person (e.g., speaker’s words, voice, image), but enhances the surrounding context (e.g., set design, editing).

- **AI Studio and Android app development**:  
  - AI Studio allows users to **vibe-code Android apps**, lowering the barrier to entry.  
  - Strategic goal: Integrate more Google products into AI Studio to reduce the need for multiple UIs.  
  - Example: A non-developer built a **gardening app** in AI Studio.  
  - Early feedback suggests it’s accelerating app development, though breakout hits have not yet emerged publicly.

### Chunk 5
- **350,000 Android apps** built in AI Studios since last week; many are personal-use apps, indicating a shift toward individualized AI software development.  
- **GenUI** may be farther out, but building personal-use software to solve individual problems is already happening at scale.  
- Android is becoming a **platform for AI builders**, especially with access to native phone capabilities and contextual data.  
- **Web vs. apps**: Web is powerful, but native OS features (e.g., richer text messaging experiences) offer capabilities that web-only AI chat apps currently lack.  
- **Model eats the scaffolding**:  
  - What was once considered the "model" (e.g., Gemini, GPT) is now a broader system including tool calling, code execution, agent harnesses, etc.  
  - Over time, scaffolding (e.g., agent harnesses, external tools) gets **integrated into the model** natively.  
  - Example: Agent harnesses are currently where alpha is, but likely to be absorbed into models within 12 months.  
- **Counterpoint on harness lock-in**:  
  - Startups build their own harnesses to avoid vendor lock-in.  
  - As model capabilities improve, the need for custom harnesses may diminish if models can natively support multiple harnesses.  
  - Suggestion: Need for a **"harness bench"** to measure how well models adapt to different harnesses.  
- **Application layer survival strategy**:  
  - Despite models eating scaffolding, startups can still thrive via **domain expertise**, vertical focus, and risk-taking.  
  - Startups have **agility and focus**, allowing them to outpace generalist model companies in niche areas.  
  - **Coding and agentic primitives** open new product categories and reduce barriers to entry.  
- **Google DeepMind culture**:  
  - Strong portfolio approach with broad investments; sometimes lags in specific domains due to lack of focus.  
  - Culture reflects **scientific rigor** and problem-solving ethos, influenced by Demis Hassabis and the original DeepMind team.  
  - Resembles the "Thinking Machines" documentary culture: smart people solving hard problems collaboratively.  
  - Contrasts with other labs like OpenAI (business-focused under Sam Altman) or Anthropic (esoteric, principled under Dario Amodei).
